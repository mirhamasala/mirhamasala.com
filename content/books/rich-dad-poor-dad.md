> What is missing from their \[people’s] education is not how to make money, but how to manage money. It’s called financial aptitude—what you do with the money once you make it, how to keep people from taking it from you, how to keep it longer, and how to make that money work hard for you.

## Changed my mind about

- Selling our Amsterdam home. I now understand that having a place that we can rent out is an asset.
- Buying a big new home. A bigger house means a bigger mortgage and higher utility bills, translating to a significant monthly expense. It’s a liability, not an asset. That’s why, instead, I’d like G and I to keep our living expenses modest and live well within our means.

## Inspired me to

- Invest my pay raise into my investment portfolio instead of indulging in new purchases
- To live more frugal by, among other things, starting a one-year no-tipping challenge, and Googling or asking for a discount before buying anything (except things like groceries)
- [Learn about sales](/books/to-sell-is-human)
