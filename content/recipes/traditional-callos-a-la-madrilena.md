## Ingredients

### Meat

| Amount | Ingredient          |
| ------ | ------------------- |
| 2 kg   | beef tripe (callos) |
| 500 g  | cow foot (patas)    |
| 300 g  | beef snout (morros) |

### Aromatics

| Amount | Ingredient           |
| ------ | -------------------- |
| 2      | onions, halved       |
| 6      | garlic cloves, whole |
| 3      | bay leaves           |
| 12     | black peppercorns    |

### Seasoning

| Amount | Ingredient                                   |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| 2 tsp  | fine sea salt (plus more to taste)           |
| 2 tbsp | pimentón dulce                               |
| ¼ tsp  | crushed red pepper flakes (or more to taste) |

### Optional but recommended

| Amount | Ingredient               |
| ------ | ------------------------ |
| 2      | Spanish chorizos, sliced |
| 1      | morcilla, sliced         |

### To serve

| Amount | Ingredient                           |
| ------ | ------------------------------------ |
|        | crusty bread                         |
|        | extra virgin olive oil               |
|        | pickled guindilla peppers (optional) |
|        | simple green salad                   |

## Method

1. Put the tripe, cow foot and beef snout in a large pot and cover with cold water. Bring to the boil and boil for 5 minutes.
2. Drain, rinse the meat under cold running water for 30 to 60 seconds and quickly rinse the pot.
3. Return the meat to the pot. Add the onions, garlic, bay leaves, peppercorns and salt. Add enough water to cover everything by 1–2 cm.
4. Bring to a gentle boil, then immediately reduce to the gentlest simmer. Cover with the lid slightly ajar and cook for 5 to 6 hours, stirring about once an hour. Add a little hot water if needed to keep everything just covered.
5. Check for doneness: the tripe should be tender, the snout soft and the cow foot almost melting. A fork should slide easily into the skin and tendons.
6. Remove and discard the onions, garlic and bay leaves.
7. Ladle 250 ml hot broth into a bowl and mix in the pimentón dulce and crushed red pepper flakes. Return the mixture to the pot.
8. If using, add the sliced chorizo and morcilla. Simmer gently for another 30 minutes.
9. Taste and adjust the salt if needed.
10. Let the callos cool, then refrigerate overnight. The broth should become noticeably thicker and often almost jelly-like.
11. Reheat gently over low heat and serve with crusty bread, good olive oil, pickled guindilla peppers and a simple green salad.

## Notes

- Start with less water: cover the ingredients by only 1–2 cm so the finished broth is rich rather than diluted.
- Do not rush the cow foot; it is the slowest component and determines when the dish is ready.
- The overnight rest is worth it. Callos is genuinely better the next day.
- For a more traditional Madrid-style heat, replace the red pepper flakes with ½–1 tsp pimentón picante.
- Cool the large batch promptly before refrigerating, dividing it between smaller containers if necessary.
