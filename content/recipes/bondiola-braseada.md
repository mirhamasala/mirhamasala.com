Sized for a 28 cm Le Creuset round casserole. Braised Friday, shredded and reheated Saturday.

---

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient                                                        |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 3 kg   | pork collar (cachaço) or shoulder, cut into 4–5 fist-sized pieces |
| 30 ml  | olive oil                                                         |
| 2      | onions, roughly chopped                                           |
| 3      | leeks, washed and thickly sliced                                  |
| 4      | carrots, thickly sliced                                           |
| 4      | garlic cloves, sliced                                             |
| 100 ml | soy sauce                                                         |
| 50 ml  | Dijon mustard                                                     |
| 50 ml  | honey                                                             |
| 50 ml  | dark muscovado sugar (açúcar mascavado), measured by volume       |
| 750 ml | beer (dark or blonde)                                             |
| 1 tsp  | salt                                                              |
| 1 tsp  | black pepper                                                      |

## Method

1. Season the pork all over with the salt and black pepper. Ideally, do this the night before the braise and leave uncovered in the fridge.
2. Heat the oven to 150 °C. Put the olive oil in the casserole over high heat and brown the pork two pieces at a time, turning to colour every side. Do not crowd the pot, as the pieces need space or they will steam. Set the browned meat aside.
3. Turn the heat to medium. Add the onions, leeks, carrots and garlic. Cook, stirring and scraping the base of the casserole, until softened and starting to colour.
4. Stir in the soy sauce, Dijon mustard, honey and muscovado sugar. Pour in the beer, bring to a simmer and let it bubble for about 1 minute.
5. Tuck the pork back into the casserole, packing the pieces snugly in a single layer. The liquid should reach roughly halfway up the meat; it does not need to cover it. Leave about 3 cm clearance below the rim.
6. Put the lid on and transfer to the oven. Turn the pieces every 45 minutes and check that there is still enough liquid, adding a splash of water if it looks dry. It is ready when a fork twists the meat apart with no resistance.
7. Let it cool, then refrigerate overnight in the casserole. The fat will set into a solid layer on top.
8. The next day, lift off most of the set fat, leaving a little behind. Warm gently on the hob until everything is steaming through.
9. Lift out the meat and shred it with two forks, discarding any large seams of fat. If the sauce is thin, boil it hard for about 10 minutes to reduce and thicken. Fold the pork back through the sauce, taste for salt and serve.

## Swaps & Substitutions

- Sugar → Honey: the 50 ml dark muscovado sugar can be replaced with 37 ml honey, and reduce the beer by about 8 ml to compensate for the added liquid. Keep an eye on the sauce as it reduces, since honey catches faster than sugar.

## Notes

- Fit: 3 kg of pork in 4–5 pieces plus 750 ml liquid sits comfortably in the 6.7 l round casserole with room at the top. Do not use two very large joints, as they sit too high and can prevent the lid from sealing properly.
- Yield: About 1.9 kg cooked meat. Plenty for 6 adults and 4 children, with a little left for sandwiches.
- Salt: The 100 ml soy sauce provides most of the seasoning, and the sauce becomes saltier as it reduces. Adjust only at the end.
- The chilli from the original recipe is omitted so the kids can eat it. Serve piri-piri at the table.
- Serve with rice and plenty of sauce. Bread also works well for a more casual meal.
