> Productivity is a trap. Becoming more efficient just makes you more rushed, and trying to clear the decks simply makes them fill up again faster.

**An exercise I undertook:** “Seek out novelty in the mundane - Pay more attention to every moment, however mundane: (...) find novelty not by doing radically different things but by plunging more deeply into the life you already have. Experience life with twice the usual intensity, and ‘your experience of life would be twice as full as it currently is.’” -Via meditation teacher Shinzen Young

> Just before sunrise, I dressed Dylan in his winter suit and went for a seaside stroll. The daybreak unfolded into a hue of pink only reserved for fishermen, dog walkers, and Wim Hoffers. I zeroed in on the coldness, allowing the cold sensations in my face to meld with the wintry air. At last, it dawned on me that my wish to live at the beach had come true. I was living the yearning. All those longings–happening. –A journal entry following the above exercise 'Seek out novelty in the mundane'

**An exercise I’d still like to undertake:** “Choose a painting or sculpture in a local museum, then go and look at it for three hours straight. No checking email or social media; no quick runs to Starbucks.” –Via Jennifer Roberts, Harvard art historian (_See: [Harvard art historian Jennifer Roberts teaches the value of immersive attention](https://harvardmagazine.com/2013/10/the-power-of-patience)_)
