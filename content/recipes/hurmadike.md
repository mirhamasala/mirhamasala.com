## Ingredients

### Dough

| Amount | Ingredient                  |
| ------ | --------------------------- |
| 2      | eggs                        |
| 240 ml | oil                         |
| 200 g  | sugar                       |
| 160 g  | desiccated coconut          |
| 10 g   | baking powder (1 sachet)    |
| 100 g  | plain flour (approximately) |

### Syrup (agda)

| Amount | Ingredient |
| ------ | ---------- |
| 800 g  | sugar      |
| 960 ml | water      |

## Method

1. Preheat the oven to 180 °C. Grease a baking tray with oil.
2. Mix all the dough ingredients together in order until a smooth mixture forms.
3. Using a grater (box grater or similar), shape the mixture into small elongated pieces directly onto the greased tray. (The soft dough is pressed through the grater to form textured cakes.)
4. Bake for about 20 minutes until golden. If they puff up too much, increase the temperature slightly.
5. While the hurmadike are baking, make the syrup: stir the sugar and water together until the sugar has dissolved. No heating is required - stir until fully combined.
6. As soon as the hurmadike come out of the oven, pour the syrup over them and leave to soak until fully absorbed.

## Swaps & Substitutions

**Sugar in the dough:** Replace the 200 g of sugar with 150 g of honey. Honey is sweeter, so you need less. The coconut flavour will still come through well. Because honey adds moisture, reduce the oil by about 30 ml.

**Sugar in the syrup:** Honey is not ideal for the syrup as it has a strong flavour and the syrup needs to be neutral to let the coconut shine. If you prefer to avoid refined sugar in the syrup, use a light agave syrup in the same volume as the water-to-sugar ratio calls for - but the traditional result is best with plain sugar syrup.

## Notes

Makes 20–24 cakes. The syrup is cold and will absorb into the hot cakes as they cool.
